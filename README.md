# 01A — Meeting Transcript → Minutes of Meeting (MoM)

### 📋 COPY PROMPT 01A

```text
Analyze the attached meeting transcript and create professional Minutes of Meeting (MoM).

Do not simply summarize the conversation. Extract and organize the important business information into:

1. Meeting objective
2. Client requirements
3. Training audience and expected number of participants
4. Topics/modules requested
5. Proposed training duration
6. Batch options discussed
7. Preferred training location
8. Alternative venue option
9. Commercial details discussed
10. Training methodology and assessments
11. Client responsibilities
12. Training provider responsibilities
13. Decisions made
14. Items still to be confirmed
15. Action items and next steps

Clearly distinguish between confirmed requirements, proposed options, and items that still need confirmation.

Do not invent any information that was not discussed in the meeting.

At the end, provide a short "Key Takeaways" section that a manager can read in less than one minute.
```

# 01B — Meeting Transcript → Client Proposal

### 📋 COPY PROMPT 01B

```text
Using the attached meeting transcript, create a professional client-ready proposal for the Excel training program.

The proposal should include:

* Executive summary
* Understanding of the client's requirement
* Training objectives
* Target audience
* Detailed training modules
* Proposed 3-day agenda
* Training methodology
* Hands-on exercises and case study
* Pre- and post-training assessment
* Participant/batch options
* Training duration
* Delivery location options
* Client responsibilities
* Training provider responsibilities
* Commercial proposal
* Optional costs
* Tentative timeline
* Assumptions
* Next steps

For the commercial section, clearly present the two scenarios discussed:

**Option 1:** Approximately 15 participants – one batch
**Option 2:** Approximately 21 participants – two batches

Clearly show how the number of trainer-days and total training cost change between the two options.

Separate the core training fees from optional external venue/hotel costs.

Use only information available in the transcript. If information required for a formal proposal has not been confirmed, mark it as "To be confirmed" rather than inventing it.

Make the final proposal polished enough to be converted directly into a professional PDF and sent to the client.
```

# 02 — Create an Executive Presentation with AI

### 📋 COPY PROMPT 02

```text
Scenario:
My manager has requested this presentation for tomorrow's management meeting. The audience consists of department heads and senior managers who understand business but are not AI experts.

Create an engaging 8–10 slide presentation that can be delivered in approximately 10–12 minutes.

Cover:

* Why AI productivity matters now
* Where employees typically spend time on repetitive work
* 5 practical ways AI can improve everyday productivity
* Examples for HR, Finance, Sales and Operations
* What should and should not be automated
* Risks such as data privacy, accuracy and over-reliance on AI
* A simple approach for introducing AI within the organization
* 3 practical actions management can take in the next 30 days
* A strong closing slide

Keep each slide simple and executive-friendly.

Avoid long paragraphs and technical AI terminology.

Use strong headlines, short supporting points, business examples, diagrams and visual storytelling wherever appropriate.

Make the presentation look like it was created by a professional management consulting team rather than a generic AI-generated presentation.

For every slide, provide:

1. Slide title
2. Key message
3. Slide content
4. Recommended visual or diagram
5. Speaker notes

Before creating the final presentation, first plan the storyline and slide sequence. Then create the presentation.
```

# 03 — AI Document Comparison

### 📋 COPY PROMPT 03

```text
I have two versions of a Software Service Agreement. 
V1 is the original we signed. 
V2 is the 'updated' version the vendor just sent, claiming only minor changes.           
Compare these two documents and identify:           
1. ALL changes between V1 and V2, categorized as: Minor wording / Commercially significant / Legally significant           
2. Any changes that reduce our rights, protections, or service levels           
3. Any NEW clauses or obligations that weren't in V1           
4. Any changes to financial terms, liability, or termination conditions           
5. A risk assessment: which changes should we push back on before signing?           

Format each change as: Section | What changed | Category | Risk level (Low/Medium/High) | Recommended action
```

# 04A — Clean Messy Business Data Using AI

### 📋 COPY PROMPT 04A

```text
1. This sheet is a messy ERP export I need cleaned up. Please:

Scan the whole sheet first — don't just look at the first few rows. These exports often repeat a junk block (blank row → section label → column headers) once per month/period throughout the entire file, not just at the top.
Remove the junk header block at the top (title, source system, report name, export date, record count, etc.) — keep only the real column headers and data.
Remove every repeated occurrence of the section-label and header rows scattered through the data body (e.g. a "January 2023" label row followed by a repeated "Invoice Date" header row before each month's data).
Delete any fully blank columns or rows — but don't touch a row just because column A is empty if other columns have data.
Split any column that combines two fields into one cell into separate columns — for example:
"Abu Dhabi, UAE" → City / Country
"Nijoe|Kabir" → Manager / Salesman
"Supermarkets:Families Co" → Customer Type / Customer Name
Before splitting, tell me the separator and proposed column names for each combined column if it's not obvious — I want to confirm before you commit.
If you find junk rows repeating somewhere I didn't mention (e.g., deeper in the sheet than I flagged), stop and ask me whether to strip all occurrences or just the ones I called out.
When done, give me the final row count and cross-check it against any 'Record Count' figure in the original metadata — they should match.
Show me a quick before/after: which columns were split into what, and which rows/columns were removed."

**************

2. Analyze the sales data Sheet "data - Clean". Give me 5 key insights with trends, in a new sheet as charts

1) Revenue & profit trend over time — monthly/quarterly Sale and Cost totals, gross margin %, and whether it's growing, flat, or declining.
2) Top and bottom performers — best and worst Manager/Salesman by total Sale and by margin.
3) Geographic breakdown — which City/Country drives the most revenue, and how that mix has shifted over the period.
4) Customer Type / Customer Name concentration — which segments or accounts contribute the most, and any customers growing or shrinking notably.
5) Product performance — best- and worst-selling products by revenue and margin, and any notable shifts across the year(s).
```
